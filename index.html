Emergency Survival Guide

🔥HeatTOP RISK

In a UK winter, cold is the biggest danger. Sort this first.

Your gas boiler probably won't work. Almost all modern boilers need electricity for the pump and controls. Having gas doesn't mean having heating.

Heat one room, not the house

  • Pick a small room with the fewest windows and outside walls.
  • Close the door. Put a rolled towel along the bottom.
  • Hang blankets or duvets over windows and the door.
  • Everyone eats, plays and sleeps in that room.
  • Keep the curtains in other rooms shut.

Keep bodies warm

  • Wear layers. Several thin layers beat one thick one.
  • Wear a hat, socks and slippers indoors, even in bed.
  • Use sleeping bags inside duvets for night time.
  • Hot water bottles are brilliant if you can boil water.
  • Eat regularly and have hot drinks. Food is fuel for your body.
  • Move about every hour to keep circulation going.

Wood burner or open fire

  • Make sure you have dry fuel for more than 3 days before winter.
  • Keep the CO alarm in the same room.
Watch for hypothermia: shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness. Warm the person gradually with blankets and warm (not hot) drinks. If they get worse, call 999.
💧Water

Mains water usually keeps running for a while, but pumping stations can fail in a long power cut.

How much to store

  • Drinking: 3 litres per person per day. This is the UK government's Be Ready advice.
  • For 3 days that's 9 litres each. A family of four needs 36 litres just for drinking.
  • Add extra for cooking and washing, and store more if you can, as the cut may last longer.
  • Don't forget pets.

Toilets

  • Fill the bath at the start.
  • If the toilet won't refill, pour a bucket of water straight into the bowl to flush.

Frozen pipes

  • A cold house can freeze pipes. Know where your stopcock is now.
  • If a pipe bursts, turn off the stopcock straight away.
If you're told water isn't safe, boil it for one minute (rolling boil) before drinking.
🥫Food and cooking

How long things stay cold

Fridge (door shut)About 4 hours
Full freezer (door shut)About 48 hours
Half full freezerAbout 24 hours

Eat fridge food first, then freezer food, then tins and dry food.

Using the cold outside

  • The garden, shed or garage can work as a fridge, but only if it stays below 5°C all day.
  • Use a cool box so animals can't get at it.

Cooking

  • A camping stove with plenty of gas canisters is the easiest option.
  • Use it outside, in a porch, or with a window wide open. CO alarm on.
  • Never leave it unattended.

No-cook Christmas backup

  • Tins you can eat cold (beans, fish, fruit, rice pudding)
  • Crackers, oatcakes, bread, cereal
  • Long-life milk and juice
  • Cheese, cured meats, peanut butter, nuts
  • Chocolate and treats for morale
The turkey: if it's in the freezer, have a plan for it in the first 2 days or accept you may lose it.
🔋Power and charging

Portable power station

  • A unit like a Jackery 300 Plus with solar panels helps, but December sun in the UK is weak. On a grey day the panels will only top it up a little.
  • Use it for phones, LED lights and the broadband router.
  • Don't use it for anything that heats (kettles, heaters, hair dryers). They drain it in minutes.
Boiler tip: a power station may run a gas boiler's pump for a couple of hours, but only if an electrician has first fitted a plug to the boiler's supply. Arrange this well before winter, never during a power cut.

Other ways to charge

  • Keep power banks fully charged at all times.
  • Your car can charge phones while the engine runs.
Never run a car engine in a closed garage, and never run a generator indoors or in a garage.
🔦Light
  • Head torches are the best buy. Both hands stay free.
  • LED lanterns for the warm room.
  • Plenty of spare batteries, all the same size if possible.
  • Keep a torch by every bed.
Keep candles to a minimum. They're a real fire risk, especially with tired people, kids and pets about. Never leave one burning in an empty room.
📻Staying in contact
  • 105 is free from any phone and gives power cut updates.
  • If your landline has switched to digital (most have), it will stop working in a power cut.
  • Mobile masts can drop out after a few hours. Send texts rather than calls, as they use less signal.
  • A wind-up or battery radio keeps you in touch with local news.
  • Agree a plan with family now: where to meet and who checks on whom if phones go down.
  • Write important numbers on paper.
💊Health and vulnerable people
  • Check which medicines need keeping cold, and plan for them.
  • Have more than a week's supply of regular medicines over Christmas. Pharmacies close.
  • Keep a first aid kit stocked.
  • Look in on elderly neighbours. Cold is the real danger for them.
Priority Services Register: if anyone at home is elderly, has young children, a disability, or uses electrical medical equipment, sign up free with your energy supplier and your local network operator. You get priority help and updates in a power cut.
💷Money and car
  • Card machines and cash machines may not work. Keep some cash at home.
  • Keep the car at least half full. Petrol pumps need power too.
  • Keep doors locked. Some alarms and gates won't work without power.
🐾Indoor pets

Pets feel the cold too, and some (fish, reptiles, birds) depend on electricity to stay alive. Plan for them like any other family member.

Pet water

  • Store water for pets on top of the family's water.
  • Rough daily guide: a cat needs about 250ml. A dog needs about 50ml per kg of body weight (a 20kg dog drinks about 1 litre).
  • Small pets like rabbits and guinea pigs need fresh water every day too. Keep spare bottles for their drinkers.
  • Water bowls and drinker spouts can freeze in a cold house. Check them morning and evening.
  • Electric water fountains will stop. Put out a normal bowl as well.

Pet food

  • Keep at least 2 weeks' food in stock over Christmas. Pet shops close and deliveries stop.
  • Dry food is the safest backup. It needs no fridge and keeps once opened.
  • Opened wet food goes off without a fridge. Buy small tins or pouches that get used in one meal.
  • Keep a tin opener that isn't electric.
  • Automatic feeders need batteries or power. Have spare batteries or feed by hand.
  • Hay for rabbits and guinea pigs: get a big bag in. It's their food and their warmth.
  • Keep treats in. They help calm a stressed pet.
  • Store food in sealed tubs so mice don't get at it.

Litter trays and toilet

  • Keep a spare bag of litter (2 weeks' worth) and spare tray liners.
  • Put the litter tray in or near the warm room. Cats may hold on rather than go to a cold room, which isn't good for them.
  • Keep a scoop, poo bags and plenty of bin bags.
  • Bin collections often change over Christmas. Double bag waste and keep it in a lidded bin outside, not in the house.
  • Dogs going out in the dark: use a head torch, plus a light-up or hi-vis collar or lead.
  • Keep puppy pads in for elderly dogs, or for times when it's too icy to go out.

Hygiene and cleaning

  • Water may be short, so keep pet-safe wipes, kitchen roll and pet-safe disinfectant spray.
  • Keep old towels for drying wet dogs. Without heating, a damp dog chills fast.
  • Wash bowls with as little water as you can. Wipe them out between meals.

Warmth and comfort

  • Bring them into your warm room with you.
  • Self-heating pet pads reflect the pet's own body heat and need no power.
  • Microwave heat pads won't reheat without power. Only count on them if they're already warm.
  • Plug-in calming diffusers stop working. Use the spray version instead.
  • A familiar blanket and toys help them settle in a dark, strange-feeling house.

Health and safety

  • Have at least 2 weeks of any pet medicine. Vets keep short hours over Christmas.
  • If any pet medicine needs keeping cold (like insulin), plan for it the same way as human medicine.
  • Write on paper: your vet's number, the out-of-hours vet number, and your pet insurance details.
  • Keep a pet first aid kit.
  • Check that microchip details are up to date and collars have ID tags. Doors open more in a power cut and scared pets bolt.
  • Keep pets away from candles and stoves. Tails and curious noses catch fire.
  • Chocolate, raisins and leftover Christmas food are poisonous to dogs and cats. It's harder to spot what they've found in the dark.
  • Have a pet carrier ready in case you need to move them somewhere warmer.

Pet supplies checklist

Dogs and cats

  • Give them blankets or a covered bed off the cold floor.
  • Older and short-haired dogs may need a coat, even indoors.
  • Keep walks short in very cold weather, but keep to their routine.
  • Cats may hide when stressed. Make sure they can't get shut in a cold room.

Rabbits, guinea pigs and small pets

  • Move cages into the warm room, off the floor and away from windows and draughts.
  • Give extra hay or bedding. It keeps them warm and fed.
  • Cover part of the cage with a blanket at night, but leave a gap for air.

Fish tanks

The filter and heater stop when the power goes. Tropical fish are most at risk.
  • Wrap the tank in blankets, towels or a duvet to hold the heat in. Polystyrene sheets work well too.
  • A battery air pump keeps oxygen in the water. Buy one before winter.
  • If you have a power station, use it for the air pump or filter (they use very little power), not the heater.
  • Feed very little or not at all for a few days. Less food means less waste fouling the water.
  • Don't do big water changes while the power is off.

Reptiles

Snakes, lizards and tortoises can't make their own heat. Without their lamps and mats they can become seriously ill quickly.
  • Keep the enclosure in your warm room and wrap it in blankets, leaving a gap for air.
  • Put hot water bottles wrapped in towels, or heat packs, outside the enclosure walls. Never let them touch the animal directly.
  • Don't feed a cold reptile. It can't digest food properly and the food can rot inside it.
  • If it's getting very cold, call your vet or local reptile shop. Some can take animals in.

Birds

  • Birds are very sensitive to fumes. Keep them well away from camping stoves, candles and cooking.
  • Move the cage away from windows and draughts, and cover it at night.
Signs a pet is too cold: shivering, being very still or sleepy, cold ears or paws, or hiding. Warm them gently and call the vet if they don't pick up.
🎲Morale

More than 3 days without power is a long time, especially at Christmas. Keeping spirits up matters more than people expect.

  • Board games, cards, puzzles, books.
  • Keep a rough daily routine: meals, a walk in daylight, bedtime.
  • Give kids small jobs. It helps them feel in control.
  • Open presents by torchlight. It can still be a good Christmas.
